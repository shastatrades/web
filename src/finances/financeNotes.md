## What is a Ledger Account?
<!--{"accordionBody":true}-->
- A Ledger Account is a collection of financial transactions
<!--{"accordionEnd":true}-->


<!--{"accordionStart":true}-->
## What is the Chart of Accounts?
<!--{"accordionBody":true}-->
- The list of every Ledger Account that we use
<!--{"accordionEnd":true}-->


<!--{"accordionStart":true}-->
## What is a Journal?
<!--{"accordionBody":true}-->
- A Journal is the chronological log of every transaction
- Ordered by date
<!--{"accordionEnd":true}-->


<!--{"accordionStart":true}-->
## What is a Ledger?
<!--{"accordionBody":true}-->
- A **journal** captures raw transactions in chronological order and a **ledger** organizes these transactions by **ledger account(s)**
- A **sub-ledger** is a Journal for a Leger Account
- A **General Ledger** is a Journal for all Ledger Accounts
<!--{"accordionEnd":true}-->


<!--{"accordionStart":true}-->
## What are the 5 financial buckets?
<!--{"accordionBody":true}-->
- Assets
- Liabilities
- Net Assets
- Revenue
- Expenses
<!--{"accordionEnd":true}-->


<!--{"accordionStart":true}-->
## What are Assets?
<!--{"accordionBody":true}-->
- An asset is **true** for all:
    1. We **own** it
    2. It has **measurable value**
    3. It will provide **future benefit** b/c it is cash OR b/c it will last beyond one year of use
- Examples:
  - Cash
  - Vehicles
  - Equipment
  - Property
  - Receivables (pledge, money owed to us)
<!--{"accordionEnd":true}-->


<!--{"accordionStart":true}-->
## What are Liabilities?
<!--{"accordionBody":true}-->
- A liability is **true** for all:
    1. It is an **obligation** to another party
    2. It arose from a **past transaction** (we already received something)
    3. It will require **future payment or performance** — either cash, services, or a deliverable
- Examples:
    - **Accounts payable:** Bills we owe to vendors
    - **Accrued payroll:** Wages earned by staff but not yet paid
    - **Payroll taxes payable:** Taxes withheld but not yet remitted
    - **Loans payable:** Mortgages, equipment loans, lines of Credit, etc.,
    - **Foundation grant with strings attached:** A liability until the conditions are met
        - Conditional grant (must meet a milestone or return it) → Liability until conditions met
        - Unconditional but restricted grant (must use for wildfire work) → Revenue with donor restriction, not a liability
<!--{"accordionEnd":true}-->


<!--{"accordionStart":true}-->
## What is Payable?
<!--{"accordionBody":true}-->
- Payable means a sum of money that must be paid
- Signifies that a debt or obligation is legally due
- Accounts payable refers to liabilities a company owes to suppliers for goods or services received on credit but not yet paid
- Example: We ordered $2,000 of lumber from a supplier. The lumber arrived. We have 30 days to pay the invoice.
- What it is not: 
    - A bill we haven't received yet (future Expense)
    - Wages owed to employees (accrued payroll)
<!--{"accordionEnd":true}-->


<!--{"accordionStart":true}-->
## What is Accrued?
<!--{"accordionBody":true}-->
- According to accrual accounting, transactions are **recorded** on the date they **incur** (occur) rather than the date **money changes hands**
- Examples:
    - Internet services used during a month are incurred as we consume them, prior to receiving or paying the monthly bill
    - Wages earned by staff in December but paid in January
<!--{"accordionEnd":true}-->


<!--{"accordionStart":true}-->
## What is Remitted?
<!--{"accordionBody":true}-->
- From a financial perspective, Remitted means to send funds (e.g., to remit the monthly invoice means to to send the money to pay the invoice)
<!--{"accordionEnd":true}-->


<!--{"accordionStart":true}-->
## What does "Withheld" mean?
<!--{"accordionBody":true}-->
- **Withheld** means money we deduct from an employee's paycheck before paying them. We hold these funds because we owe it (must send the funds to) the government
- The employee never sees this money in their bank account. We keep it, and later **remit** it to the IRS and the EDD on their behalf
- **Withheld is not our money.** It is the employee's money that we are holding in trust. We are a **pass-through** between the employee's paycheck and the tax agencies
- **Withholding is legally required.** If we fail to withhold, or withhold and fail to remit, **we are liable** — not the employee
<!--{"accordionEnd":true}-->


<!--{"accordionStart":true}-->
## Which taxes are "Withheld" and which are "Employer-paid"?
<!--{"accordionBody":true}-->
| Tax | Who pays it | Is it withheld from the employee? |
|---|---|---|
| **Federal income tax** | Employee | **Yes — withheld** |
| **California Personal Income Tax (PIT)** | Employee | **Yes — withheld** |
| **Social Security** | Split 50/50 | **Yes — employee half withheld**; employer pays the other half directly |
| **Medicare** | Split 50/50 | **Yes — employee half withheld**; employer pays the other half directly |
| **State Disability Insurance (SDI)** | Employee | **Yes — withheld** |
| **Federal Unemployment (FUTA)** | Employer only | **No — employer pays directly** |
| **California Unemployment (UI)** | Employer only | **No — employer pays directly** |
| **Employment Training Tax (ETT)** | Employer only | **No — employer pays directly** |

- **Withheld taxes** appear as a **liability** (we owe them to the government) until we remit them
- **Employer-paid taxes** appear as an **expense** from day one, because they are our cost, not the employee's
<!--{"accordionEnd":true}-->


<!--{"accordionStart":true}-->
## What are Net Assets?
<!--{"accordionBody":true}-->
- What we truly own
- What we own after we pay our Liabilities
- **Net Assets = Assets - Liabilities**
- Example:
    - We buy a **$500,000 office** with a **$400,000 mortgage** and **$100,000** cash down payment
    - **Asset:** What we own = $500,000 office
    - **Liability:** What you owe = $400,000 mortgage
    - **Net Asset:** What we truly own = $100,000
<!--{"accordionEnd":true}-->


<!--{"accordionStart":true}-->
## How do Nonprofits split Net Assets?
<!--{"accordionBody":true}-->
- Net assets **without** donor restrictions:
    - Money our Board can spend freely (e.g., General Operating, Operating Reserve, Capital & Equipment)
- Net assets **with** donor restrictions:
    - Money legally restricted by Donors (e.g., Wildfire Repair, Elderly Assistance, Opportunity Youth)
<!--{"accordionEnd":true}-->



<!--{"accordionStart":true}-->
## What is Revenue?
<!--{"accordionBody":true}-->
- Value that comes in from outside our oganization (like a liability), with no obligation to give something back (unlike a liability)
- **Examples for Shasta Trades:**
    - Donations
    - Grants
    - Service fees (trades invoices)
    - In-kind gifts
<!--{"accordionEnd":true}-->


<!--{"accordionStart":true}-->
## What is an Expense?
<!--{"accordionBody":true}-->
- The cost of resources consumed to fulfill our mission
- Examples:
    - **Program Expenses:** Apprentice wages, mentor wages, program materials, tools consumed on jobs
    - **Admin Expenses:** Rent, insurance, software, salary, Board meetings
    - **Fundraising Expenses:** Donor emails, grant writing, event costs, thank-you cards
- Every Expense gets one functional class:
    - `mission`
    - `admin`
    - `fundraising`
<!--{"accordionEnd":true}-->


<!--{"accordionStart":true}-->
## What is a Functional Class?
<!--{"accordionBody":true}-->
- A Functional Class is a column on the transaction's table that answers "Was this spending for our mission, for admin, or for fundraising?"
- Required by the IRS on **Form 990 Part IX** (Statement of Functional Expenses)
- Every transaction gets exactly one functional class
- Helps us tell the IRS (and our Board) whether each dollar we spent was for `mission`, `admin`, or `fundraising`
    | Class | Meaning | Examples |
    |---|---|---|
    | `mission` | Directly advances the mission | Apprentice wages, mentor wages, tools & materials |
    | `admin` | Keeps the lights on | Rent, insurance, accounting software, CTO salary, Board meetings |
    | `fundraising` | Raises money | Donor emails, grant writing, event costs, thank-you cards |
<!--{"accordionEnd":true}-->


<!--{"accordionStart":true}-->
## What is Receivable?
<!--{"accordionBody":true}-->
- Receivable tracks assets owed to us for goods or services we have already provided
- Examples:
    - Sarah (the realtor) hired us for a $4,000 kitchen rehab. The work is done. **$4,000 in accounts receivable**
    - A grant funder approved $25,000 and the award letter is signed, but the check hasn't arrived. → **$25,000 in 1110 · Grants Receivable** (a subtype of AR)
    - A donor pledged $10,000 and signed a written promise. No cash yet. → **Pledges Receivable** (a subtype of AR)
- What it is not: 
    - Not a promise someone might make someday (prospect)
    - Not cash in the bank (cash)
- Just remember that Payable & Receivable sound similair b/c they are, **payable** is when we are scheduled to **pay** and **receivable** is when we are scheduled to **receive**
<!--{"accordionEnd":true}-->

## What is a Transaction?
<!--{"accordionBody":true}-->
- A single economic event
- Transaction Formula:
    1. List each transaction line
        - Every transaction has at least 2 lines
        - Every line has:
            - Amount
            - Account (e.g., `1010 · Checking`)
            - Fund (e.g., `General Operating`)
            - Direction
                - Asset: Did we **gain** or **lose**?
                - Liability: We owe **more** or **less**?
                - Expense: We consume **more** or **less**?
                - Net Asset: Did we **gain** or **lose**?
                - Revenue: We earn **more** or **less**?
    2. Set Credit / Debit for each line:
        - **All Elephants Dance**
            - Increasing Assets or Expenses = Debit
        - **Little Ninjas Ride Chickens**
            - Increaseing Liabilities, Net Assets or Revenue = Credit
<!--{"accordionEnd":true}-->


<!--{"accordionStart":true}-->
## What is Credit?
<!--{"accordionBody":true}-->
- From Latin's *creditum*
- No connection to "credit card"
- In every transaction, **total debits must equal total credits**
- **Credit** = Where did the value come from? **Debit** = Where is the value now?
- Just remember C comes before D **alphabetically** and **financially**, examples:
    - Paid Wages:
        | Account | Entry |
        |---|---|
        | `1010 · Checking` | $10,000 **Credit** |
        | `5030 · Administrative Salaries` | $10,000 **Debit** |
    - Bought Tools w/ a Credit Card:
        | Account | Entry |
        |---|---|
        | `2020 · Credit Card Payable` | $500 **Credit** |
        | `5210 · Tools & Consumables` | $500 **Debit** |
<!--{"accordionEnd":true}-->


<!--{"accordionStart":true}-->
## What is Debit?
<!--{"accordionBody":true}-->
- From Latin's *debitum*
- No connection to "debit card"
- In every transaction, **total debits must equal total credits**
- **Credit** = Where did the value come from? **Debit** = Where is the value now?
- Just remember C comes before D **alphabetically** and **financially**, examples:
    - Received a Grant:
        | Account | Entry |
        |---|---|
        | `4030 · Foundation Grants` | $50,000 **Credit** |
        | `1010 · Checking` | $50,000 **Debit** |
    - Purchased a Vehicle w/ Cash:
        | Account | Entry |
        |---|---|
        | `1010 · Checking` | $30,000 **Credit** |
        | `1500 · Vehicles` | $30,000 **Debit** |
    - Monthly Vehicle Depreciation:
        | Account | Entry |
        |---|---|
        | `1510 · Vehicles Accumulated Depreciation` | $500 **Credit** |
        | `5700 · Depreciation Expense` | $500 **Debit** |
        - Explanation:
            - $30,000 vehicle purchase price, 5 year useful life, $6,000/year, $500/month
            - Vehicle is $500 less valuable b/c we used $500 of its value this month
    - Monthly Office Depreciation:
        | Account | Entry |
        |---|---|
        | `1810 · Building Accumulated Depreciation` | $1,000 **Credit** |
        | `5700 · Depreciation Expense` | $1,000 **Debit** |
        - Explanation:
            - $500,000 office, $468,000 building, $32,000 land **(land does not depreciate)**
            - $468,000 building, 39 year useful life (standard) = $1,000/month
            - Office is $1,000 less valuable b/c we used $1,000 of its value this month
<!--{"accordionEnd":true}-->

## What is a Balance Sheet?
<!--{"accordionBody":true}-->
- Financial report for a point (moment) in time
- Never a date range
- Equation:
    - Net Assets = Assets - Liabilities
        - What we truly own = (What we own) - (What we owe)
        - Example: $100,000 down payment = $500,000 house - $400,000 mortgage
- The Balance Sheet layout matches the **Equation**
    - Assets = Net Assets + Liabilities
        - What we own = (What we truly own) + (What we owe)
        - Example: $500,000 house = $100,000 down payment + $400,000 mortgage
    - **Left side:** Assets
    - **Right side:** Net Assets + Liabilities
    - The two sides must be equal
        - That is why it is called a *balance* sheet
<!--{"accordionEnd":true}-->


<!--{"accordionStart":true}-->
## What is an Income Statement?
<!--{"accordionBody":true}-->
- Statement of Activities
- Tracks Revenue and Expenses over a period (e.g., month, quarter, year)
- Equation:
    - Beginning Net Assets + Revenue − Expenses = Ending Net Assets
        - (What we truly owned at the start) + (What came in) − (What we used up) = What we truly own at the end
        - $60,000 (Beginning Net Assets) + $210,000 (Revenue) - $180,000 (Expenses) = $90,000 (Ending Net Assets)
- Answers: *"How richer or poorer did we become during this period?"*
    - Surplus: Revenue > Expenses
    - Deficit: Revenue < Expenses
<!--{"accordionEnd":true}-->


<!--{"accordionStart":true}-->
## How do a Balance Sheet and Income Statement relate?
<!--{"accordionBody":true}-->
- Balance Sheet:
    - Snapshot equation
    - Net Assets = Assets - Liabilities
    - Describes where we stand on a day
    - **Start-of-period Balance Sheet:** (e.g., January 1, 2026) provides beginning of period Net Assets
    - **End-of-period Balance Sheet:** (e.g., December 31, 2026) provides end of period Net Assets
- Income Statement:
    - Period equation
    - Beginning Net Assets + Revenue - Expenses = Ending Net Assets
    - Describes how our wealth changed during a period
    - Uses the **beginning net assets** as its starting input and produces **ending net assets** as its output
- The Income Statement is the **bridge** between the starting period and ending period Balance Sheets

## What is Depreciation?
<!--{"accordionBody":true}-->
- Depreciation is the way we spread the cost of a long-lasting asset across the years it actually helps us
- The problem it solves:
    - We buy a $30,000 truck on January 21 and pay all $30,000 in cash that day. But the truck serves the mission for **five years**. If we recorded the whole $30,000 as an expense in January, that one month would look terrible and the next four years would look free. Neither is true
    - Depreciation fixes the timing: We record **$6,000 of expense each year for five years**, so each year carries its fair share of the truck's cost
- **The rule:**
    - IF an asset will help us for **more than one year** AND costs at least our capitalization threshold (**$2,500**) THEN we **capitalize** it on the balance sheet and **depreciate** it over its useful life
- Depreciation is an allocation of a past payment, not a new payment
<!--{"accordionEnd":true}-->


<!--{"accordionStart":true}-->
## What is Useful Life?
<!--{"accordionBody":true}-->
- Useful life is how many years we expect the asset to serve us before it needs replacing
- Examples:
    | Asset | Useful life |
    |---|---|
    | Laptops, software equipment | 3 years |
    | Power tools, hand tools capitalized | 3–5 years |
    | Vehicles (truck, van, trailer) | 5–7 years |
    | Furniture | 7 years |
    | Building | 39 years (IRS convention for nonresidential real property) |
    | **Land** | **Never depreciated** |
<!--{"accordionEnd":true}-->


<!--{"accordionStart":true}-->
## What is Accumulated Depreciation?
<!--{"accordionBody":true}-->
- Accumulated Depreciation is the running total of all depreciation we have ever recorded on an asset, from the day we bought it until today
- Depreciation expense is **this period's slice**
- Accumulated depreciation is **every slice ever taken, added up**
<!--{"accordionEnd":true}-->

## What is an In-Kind donation?
<!--{"accordionBody":true}-->
- A gift of **goods, services, property, or use of facilities** instead of cash
- Still a donation for accounting, receipting, and 990/199 purposes
    - **Value came in from somewhere** → record **in-kind revenue**
    - **Value now sits somewhere** → record an **asset** or an **expense**
- Has a **fair market value (FMV)** that must be documented, not guessed
- Can be **restricted** (e.g., “this laptop must be used for apprentice training”) or unrestricted
- Two big categories: **goods/property** (laptops, tools, trucks, materials) and **services** (pro bono legal, accounting, trades labor)
- **In-kind types:**
    | Type | Examples | What Makes It This Type | Books As |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | `vehicles` | Truck, van, car, trailer, heavy equipment with a title or registration | **Titled or registered** motor vehicle or towable | Fixed asset; **Form 1098-C** if claimed value > $500; **Form 8283** |
    | `equipmentAndFurniture` | Laptops, printers, monitors, desks, chairs, shelving, power tools, hand tools, large tool sets, generators, compressors | **Durable property with useful life > 1 year**, not a vehicle, not a building | Fixed asset if **≥ capitalization threshold**; otherwise **expense when placed in service** |
    | `consumableSupplies` | Paint, lumber, fasteners, caulk, sandpaper, safety gear (gloves, masks, harnesses), PPE, cleaning supplies | **Used up or consumed within a single job or season** | Expense when used |
    | `inventoryForResale` | Donated building materials Shasta Trades **intends to sell** to fund programs | Donated goods held **primarily for sale**, not for program use | Inventory asset; cost of goods sold when sold |
    | `realProperty` | Donated land, donated building, donated easement | **Real estate transferred outright** (title changes hands) | Fixed asset; appraisal required; **Form 8283 Section B** |
    | `useOfFacilities` | Free office space, free warehouse, free training bay, free storage | **Right to use space without owning it** | In-kind rent revenue + rent expense (equal amounts) |
    | `professionalServices` | Pro bono legal, accounting, marketing, consulting, engineering, IT, grant writing | **Specialized knowledge-based service** | Recognize **only if** specialized + skilled provider + would otherwise purchase; otherwise **disclose only** |
    | `tradesServices` | Mentor donates 10 hours of electrical work; contractor donates a plumbing repair | **Skilled physical trade labor** | Same three-part test as professional services; usually **disclose only** |
    | `securities` | Stocks, bonds, mutual funds, ETFs, crypto | **Financial instrument** with a market value | Investment asset; **Form 8283**; appraisal not needed for publicly traded securities |
    | `intangibleAssets` | Software licenses, domain names, trademarks, patents, copyrights, royalty rights | **Non-physical asset with legal or contractual value** | Asset if ≥ threshold; otherwise expense or disclose |
    | `foodAndEventGoods` | Donated catering, beverages, auction items, event supplies | Goods donated **for a specific event** | Expense in the period of the event; watch **quid pro quo** disclosure |
<!--{"accordionEnd":true}-->

## What is FMV (Fair Market Value)?
<!--{"accordionBody":true}-->
- **FMV** stands for **Fair Market Value**
- It is **the price that a willing buyer would pay a willing seller** for the same item, when neither is under pressure to buy or sell, and both know what they are trading
- It is **not** what the donor originally paid, **not** what the item is worth to us emotionally, and **not** what we hope to sell it for
- **Why we need it:** The IRS requires a dollar value for every in-kind donation so the donor can claim a deduction and so we can record revenue and expense correctly
- **Examples:**
    | Item | Donor paid | FMV today | Why |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | A 3-year-old laptop | $1,200 new | **$300** | Used laptops of that age and spec sell for ~$300 on eBay |
    | A used pickup truck | $25,000 new | **$12,000** | Kelley Blue Book private-party value in current condition |
    | A hammer | $30 new | **$30** | Brand new, still in packaging |
    | 10 hours of an attorney’s time | Billed at $350/hr | **$3,500** | That is what she charges clients |
    | Free office space, 500 sq ft | — | **$1,500/month** | Comparable office space rents for $3/sq ft in that area |
<!--{"accordionEnd":true}-->

## What is Pro Bono?
<!--{"accordionBody":true}-->
- **Pro bono** is short for **pro bono publico**—Latin for **“for the public good”**
- It means **professional services donated free of charge or at a substantially reduced rate** to a nonprofit or to people who cannot afford them
- **Typical examples:** an attorney drafting bylaws for free, a CPA preparing a Form 990 at no charge, a marketing firm designing a campaign for free, a structural engineer reviewing a repair plan
- **Pro bono vs volunteer work:** Volunteer work is **any** unpaid help (painting a wall, handing out flyers). Pro bono is **unpaid work that uses a person’s professional skill**—the same service they normally charge for
<!--{"accordionEnd":true}-->

## What is a Jurisdiction?
<!--{"accordionBody":true}-->
- A jurisdiction is a government authority that has the legal power to require filings, collect taxes, or enforce compliance
- Shasta Trades answers to several layers of government at the same time
- Each jurisdiction has its own forms, deadlines, penalties, and rules
<!--{"accordionEnd":true}-->


<!--{"accordionStart":true}-->
## What is Gross Receipts?
<!--{"accordionBody":true}-->
- **Gross receipts** is the **total cash or total amount received** from all sources **before any deductions for costs, expenses, or returns**
- For a fundraising event, gross receipts is simply the **total cash generated**—e.g., 100 attendees at $100 each = **$10,000 in gross receipts**
- For a nonprofit organization overall, gross receipts includes **contributions, gifts, grants, program service revenue, and other income—without subtracting the expenses of raising or collecting that money**
- **Gross receipts is the threshold metric** used to determine which **IRS Form 990 series** a nonprofit must file:
    - **≤ $50,000** → Form 990-N (e-Postcard)
    - **< $200,000** → Form 990-EZ
    - **≥ $200,000** → Full Form 990
- Gross receipts is the **total cash in**; gross income is the **value of what was given back** in exchange
<!--{"accordionEnd":true}-->


<!--{"accordionStart":true}-->
## What is Gross Income?
<!--{"accordionBody":true}-->
- **Gross income** is the **fair market value (FMV) of the goods or services** that a person or organization receives **in exchange for a payment**—not the total cash paid
- In nonprofit accounting, gross income is most often relevant for **fundraising events and quid pro quo contributions**
- **Example:** A charity dinner ticket costs **$100**. The attendee receives a meal with a retail value of **$30**. The **gross income** is **$30 per attendee** (the value of what they received), not the full $100 ticket price
- **Why it matters:** The portion of the payment that exceeds gross income is the **tax-deductible contribution**. In the example, **$70 per attendee** is a contribution and **$30 is gross income** (the value of the benefit received)
- Gross receipts is the **total cash in**; gross income is the **value of what was given back** in exchange
<!--{"accordionEnd":true}-->


<!--{"accordionStart":true}-->
## What is UBTI?
<!--{"accordionBody":true}-->
- **UBTI** stands for **Unrelated Business Taxable Income**
- In plain terms: IF Shasta Trades earns money that is **not substantially related** to our mission THEN that income is UBTI
- UBTI is **taxable** at corporate rates and is reported on **IRS Form 990-T** (federal) and **California Form 109** (state)
<!--{"accordionEnd":true}-->


<!--{"accordionStart":true}-->
## What is an Unemployment Tax Return?
<!--{"accordionBody":true}-->
- An **Unemployment Tax Return** is the filing that reports **wages paid to employees** and the **unemployment insurance (UI) tax** owed on those wages
- **Federal:** **Form 940** (Employer’s Annual Federal Unemployment Tax Return) reports **FUTA** tax. FUTA is **2.1%** (after the 5.4% state credit) on the first **$7,000** of wages per employee[reference:3]
- **California:** **Form DE 9** and **Form DE 9C** are the **quarterly** unemployment and wage reports filed with the **Employment Development Department (EDD)** . **DE 9** reports contributions and **DE 9C** reports wages[reference:4]
<!--{"accordionEnd":true}-->


<!--{"accordionStart":true}-->
## What is Backup Withholding?
<!--{"accordionBody":true}-->
- IF we pay an independent contractor (e.g., a consultant, a guest speaker, or a tradesperson treated as a 1099 contractor) and do not have a valid **W-9** with a correct TIN, THEN backup withholding may apply
- **Backup withholding** is a **24% federal income tax withholding** that a payer (Shasta Trades) must apply to certain payments when the payee (e.g., a consultant, a guest speaker, or a tradesperson treated as a 1099 contractor) **fails to provide a correct Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN)** or fails to certify that they are not subject to backup withholding
- It is a **compliance enforcement mechanism**: if the IRS cannot match a payment to a correct TIN, the payer must withhold tax and send it to the IRS
- **Shasta Trades’ obligation:** Collect a valid **Form W-9** from every contractor **before paying them** to avoid backup withholding and to ensure proper 1099-NEC reporting
<!--{"accordionEnd":true}-->


<!--{"accordionStart":true}-->
## What is Nonemployee Compensation?
<!--{"accordionBody":true}-->
- **Nonemployee compensation** is money paid to someone who is **not on payroll as W-2 employees** for services performed for the organization’s trade or business
- The general reporting threshold is **$600 or more** paid to a single payee during the calendar year
- If Shasta Trades **controls what work is done, how it is done, and when it is done**, the worker is generally an **employee**, and wages are reported on **Form W-2**, not **1099-NEC**
<!--{"accordionEnd":true}-->


<!--{"accordionStart":true}-->
## What is ERISA?
<!--{"accordionBody":true}-->
- **ERISA** stands for the **Employee Retirement Income Security Act of 1974**
- It is the federal law that sets minimum standards for **most voluntarily established pension and health plans** in private industry, including **nonprofit employers**
- ERISA requires plan administrators to provide **participants with plan information**, sets **fiduciary responsibilities**, and establishes **funding and vesting rules**
- ERISA applies if the nonprofit sponsors a **retirement plan** (e.g., a 401(k), 403(b), pension, or welfare benefit plan)
- If Shasta Trades only pays wages and does not sponsor a funded employee benefit plan then ERISA/Form 5500 does not apply
<!--{"accordionEnd":true}-->


<!--{"accordionStart":true}-->
## What are Employee Plan Returns?
<!--{"accordionBody":true}-->
- **Employee plan returns** are the annual **information returns** that an employer must file for certain **employee benefit plans** it sponsors
- The main form is the **Form 5500 series** (Annual Return/Report of Employee Benefit Plan), which reports **plan participants, funding, investments, and compliance**
- **Who files:** The employer maintaining the plan or the plan administrator of a **pension or welfare benefit plan covered by ERISA**
- If we offer a **401(k), 403(b), or other funded retirement plan**, Form 5500 is likely required
- If we only offer a payroll deduction IRA or offer no plans then filing may not apply
<!--{"accordionEnd":true}-->


<!--{"accordionStart":true}-->
## What is FTB?
<!--{"accordionBody":true}-->
- **FTB** stands for the **Franchise Tax Board**
— California’s state tax agency responsible for administering **exempt organization filings**
- **Key FTB Forms:**
    - **Form 199:** California Exempt Organization Annual Information Return, the state equivalent of Form 990, required if **gross receipts > $50,000**
    - **FTB 199N:** California e-Postcard, for organizations with **gross receipts normally ≤ $50,000**
    - **Form 109:** California Exempt Organization Business Income Tax Return, for **California UBTI > $1,000**
    - **FTB 3500 / 3500A:** Applications for California tax-exempt status
<!--{"accordionEnd":true}-->

## What is CDTFA?
<!--{"accordionBody":true}-->
- **CDTFA** stands for the **California Department of Tax and Fee Administration**
- Administers **sales and use tax**, **excise taxes**, and certain **fee programs** in California
- There is **no general sales tax exemption** for nonprofit organizations in California. Nonprofits may need a **seller’s permit** and may owe sales/use tax on taxable sales or purchases
- **Common CDTFA Forms:**
    - **CDTFA-230:** Resale Certificate, for buying goods intended for resale
    - **CDTFA-101:** Exemption Certificate, for qualified exempt purchases
    - **CDTFA-230-C-2:** For qualified nonprofit facility construction exemptions
- **For Shasta Trades:** If the nonprofit sells donated goods, materials, or merchandise, or makes taxable purchases, CDTFA compliance may apply. Do **not** assume “nonprofit = no sales tax.”
<!--{"accordionEnd":true}-->


<!--{"accordionStart":true}-->
## What is EDD?
<!--{"accordionBody":true}-->
- **EDD** stands for the **Employment Development Department**
- California’s state agency that administers **payroll taxes**, including **Unemployment Insurance (UI)**, **State Disability Insurance (SDI)**, **Employment Training Tax (ETT)**, and **Personal Income Tax (PIT) withholding**
    - **UI** = **Unemployment Insurance:** An **employer-paid** tax that funds unemployment benefits for workers who lose their jobs. The wage limit is **$7,000** per employee per year
    - **ETT** = **Employment Training Tax:** An **employer-paid** tax that funds worker training programs. The wage limit is **$7,000** per employee per year
    - **SDI** = **State Disability Insurance:** An **employee-paid** tax (withheld from wages) that provides short-term disability and paid family leave benefits. There is **no wage limit** as of 2024
    - **PIT** = **Personal Income Tax:** California’s **state income tax** that employers **withhold from employee wages** and remit to the EDD. There is **no wage limit**
<!--{"accordionEnd":true}-->


<!--{"accordionStart":true}-->
## What is a Resale Certificate?
<!--{"accordionBody":true}-->
- A **resale certificate** is a document a **buyer** gives a **seller** to purchase goods **without paying sales tax** because the buyer intends to **resell** those goods in the regular course of business
- In California, the common form is **CDTFA-230** (Resale Certificate)
- **Who can use it:** Retailers and wholesalers that buy inventory for resale. It is **not** a general nonprofit exemption.
- If we purchase **building materials or goods that it will resell** to clients as part of a program (e.g., selling materials at cost as part of a repair project), a resale certificate **may** apply to those specific purchases
If we are a **consumer** of materials (using them in our repair work rather than reselling them), a resale certificate generally **does not apply**
<!--{"accordionEnd":true}-->


<!--{"accordionStart":true}-->
## What is Quid Pro Quo?
<!--{"accordionBody":true}-->
- **Quid pro quo** is Latin for **“something for something”**
- In nonprofit fundraising, a **quid pro quo contribution** is a payment made by a donor **partly as a charitable contribution** and **partly in exchange for goods or services** provided by the organization
- **Example:** A donor gives Shasta Trades **$100** and receives a **$40** benefit (e.g., a dinner, a gift, or event admission). The **quid pro quo contribution** is **$100**; the **deductible portion** is **$60** ($100 minus the $40 value received)
- **Disclosure requirement:** If the payment **exceeds $75**, Shasta Trades must provide a **written disclosure statement** that:
    1. States the amount of the payment that is **deductible**, and
    2. Provides a **good-faith estimate** of the value of goods or services provided
<!--{"accordionEnd":true}-->


<!--{"accordionStart":true}-->
## What is Excise Tax?
<!--{"accordionBody":true}-->
- An **excise tax** is a tax imposed on the **manufacture, sale, or use of specific goods, services, or activities**, rather than on income or property
- **In California sales tax context:** California imposes an **excise tax on retailers** for the **privilege of selling tangible personal property at retail**. This tax is **computed as a percentage of gross receipts** and is the legal basis for the sales tax that retailers collect from customers
<!--{"accordionEnd":true}-->


<!--{"accordionStart":true}-->
## What is Use Tax?
<!--{"accordionBody":true}-->
- **Use tax** is the **companion tax to California sales tax**. It applies when **California sales tax was not paid** on a taxable purchase, but the item is **used, stored, or consumed in California**
- **Common triggers for use tax:**
    - Purchasing taxable items from an **out-of-state vendor** (e.g., online, mail order) that does **not collect California sales tax**
    - Removing items from **inventory** and using them in California **without having paid tax** at purchase
- **How it is paid:** Use tax is reported on the organization’s **sales and use tax return** under **“Purchases Subject to Use Tax.”** The purchase price is added to the total amount subject to tax
- **There is no general exemption from use tax for nonprofit organizations.** If Shasta Trades buys tools, equipment, or materials from an out-of-state seller that does not collect California tax, and those items are used in California, **use tax is generally owed**
<!--{"accordionEnd":true}-->
